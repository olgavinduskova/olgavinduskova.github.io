Olga Vindušková

I am an assistant professor at Charles University. I love to study nature and soils with all my senses. My research interests usually live in the ecotone between soil science and ecosystem ecology. My research is often about what shapes soils and/or what shape are they in, I'm particularly interested in soil carbon dynamics, soil development and how are these affected by humans and climate. In my free time, I like to move, meet other monkeys, read and look for ways to make the world a (even) better place.

Soil carbon dynamics under climate change

Understanding how climate change alters soil carbon (SOC) storage requires integrating changes in precipitation regimes, drought frequency, temperature, and plant-derived carbon inputs with belowground biotic processes. My research combines field, mesocosm and laboratory experiments to examine how climate change affects soil carbon dynamics. Building on my work at University of Antwerp and within the current DrySoilCarbon project (Czech Science Foundation, 2026–2028), I investigate how future drought, elevated CO₂, and shifts in carbon inputs influence long-term carbon persistence in temperate ecosystems.

Modelling soil carbon dynamics

To mechanistically predict soil carbon responses to environmental change, I develop and refine process-based models that explicitly represent measurable carbon pools and biotic interactions. Within the SOMForClim project, I co-developed a model based on KEYLINK, incorporating particulate and mineral-associated organic matter fractions (POC, MAOC) and priming effects to improve predictions of SOC dynamics under varying carbon inputs and warming scenarios. This modelling work bridges experimental data with theory, enabling quantitative forecasts of soil carbon turnover under future climate conditions.

Soil carbon dynamics during ecosystem development

I investigate how soil carbon accumulates and stabilizes during ecosystem development following natural and anthropogenic disturbances. Using chronosequences of landslides and post-mining landscapes, including long-term research sites in northwest Czechia, I examine how parent material, vegetation, and reclamation strategies regulate SOC formation and persistence. By combining field studies with global syntheses, this work reveals general controls on carbon dynamics during pedogenesis and ecosystem recovery, providing a long-term perspective on soil carbon vulnerability and resilience.
